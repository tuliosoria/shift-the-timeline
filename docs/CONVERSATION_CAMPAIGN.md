# Conversation progression campaign

This campaign builds on main `857e06b3d410a9a7f592c8e324710865098c4d4c` (PR #49). It preserves the phone-first private interviews, existing factual safeguards, medium reasoning, provider transport limits, canonical fallback, and all question/turn rules. Production financial policy is unchanged.

## Behaviour

An admitted AI question snapshots only this player's settled exchanges with the selected suspect in this room. Prior visits now contribute context. The job retains up to eleven whole exchanges, favouring recent exchanges and an early anchor, while measuring the complete stored Item and leaving 1 KiB below its enlarged 64 KiB limit for later job lifecycle fields. It omits whole exchanges that cannot fit; it never invents summaries. Pending answers are excluded. An old answer settling after a new question's admission cannot rewrite that question's immutable context. A later question can remember the newly settled answer.

Already-earned, suspect-scoped deduction IDs remove those cards from the remaining award candidates shown to the model. The complete authored brief and canonical responses remain available. The resolver's full allowed scope and duplicate filtering are unchanged. Every new award still requires all of its facts in the current reply; prior conversation cannot complete a partial clue. This avoids repeatedly encouraging a performance of a card the player already owns.

The prompt asks for a direct answer first, economical follow-up progression and behaviour grounded in the authored persona's priorities and permitted evasions. It distinguishes an acknowledgement, a request to repeat, an unknown fact and a genuine ambiguity. It keeps internal checking language out of spoken dialogue. These are probabilistic instructions, not a guarantee of factual or conversational quality.

## Storage size and compatibility

The former 16,000-byte AI Item cap was self-imposed, not a database limit. It is raised to **65,536 bytes (64 KiB)**. This is above the current 20,000-byte provider context even after nested JSON escaping and normal job metadata, so durable storage no longer dictates ordinary interview memory. The remaining provider request guard and history fitting preserve the existing per-request model policy and bounded work; they are operational choices, not the model's maximum context size.

DynamoDB permits [400 KB per item](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/amazondynamodb/latest/developerguide/Constraints.html) and [4 MB across a transaction](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/amazondynamodb/latest/APIReference/API_TransactWriteItems.html). PostgreSQL documents a [1 GB field limit](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/16/limits.html); Shift uses an unindexed JSONB data field and has no 16 KB database constraint. Both adapters call the same codec for AI writes. Queue messages and due indexes carry job keys, not full prompts. The larger operational guard keeps clear headroom below storage limits and bounds repeated lifecycle writes, without treating those ceilings as desirable prompt sizes.

No record, schema, index or version format changes, and no database migration is needed. Existing jobs remain readable and processable. **Rollback caveat:** an older binary with the 16 KB codec cannot update a newly created larger job. Preserve the enlarged codec when rolling back conversational behaviour, or verify all larger pending jobs have settled before returning to an old worker. A timer alone is not proof that the durable queue drained. The existing Azure deployment mechanism is unchanged.

## Reproducible paid evaluation

`scripts/evaluate-conversations.mjs` runs each variant through that revision's actual `DurableGame`, `AiJobs`, history admission, worker and deduction settlement. Three local synthetic players establish a legal room; one asks the fixed scripted questions over legal visits. Each arm's own replies become its own history. A fresh room is used for each conversation. No private case, teammate conversation, reviewer rubric or expected answer enters model input.

The public-demo development and held-out plans each contain eight six-question conversations: two three-question visits, with later revisits. The held-out wording remains sealed until the candidate is frozen. Reviewers compare anonymous paired transcripts for directness, unnecessary repetition, contextual follow-ups, authored voice, unknown facts, accusations and injection handling. Factual/provenance/chronology/spoiler/card failures are scored separately. Synthetic transport or browser tests do not constitute naturalness evidence.

Every real request needs a persistent campaign ledger reservation before provider invocation. The ledger is shared across runs and process restarts. Pending or uncertain attempts retain their full reservation; successful verified token receipts settle the exact rounded cost. No automatic paid retries. The runner stops after three consecutive fallbacks. Ledger corruption, missing state, a changed cap/rate, duplicate request ID, overspend or a stale lock fails closed. Never delete or reinitialise the ledger to continue spending.

The approved execution allowance is **$4.50 total**, with **$0.50 outside that ledger reserved for coordinated final verification**. Model `gpt-6-luna`, Standard rates $0.10/input and $0.50/output per million tokens, were checked against [OpenAI's model documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-6-luna). Production retains its 6,000/1,024-token policy. Campaign testing reserves at least 5,000 microUSD per attempted request, including uncertain failures: the 20,000-byte input guard is not a proof of a 6,000-token maximum. This larger evaluation allowance covers the current byte-bounded request and schema overhead at the approved rates without changing production settings. Counts and calculated usage costs are not provider billing receipts. Every live browser question must also be charged conservatively to the campaign allowance.

Initialise one ledger explicitly using `createLedger` from `scripts/evaluation-ledger.mjs`, an absolute normalised path, cap `4500000`, and rates `{inputRate:0.1, outputRate:0.5}`. Subsequent runs use `openLedger`. Keep the ledger and raw transcripts outside tracked source or under ignored `.tmp/`.

The CLI requires `SHIFT_EVAL_LEDGER`, `SHIFT_EVAL_PLAN`, `SHIFT_EVAL_ROOT`, `SHIFT_EVAL_RUN_ID`, `SHIFT_EVAL_VARIANT`, `SHIFT_EVAL_REVISION`, `SHIFT_EVAL_OUTPUT` and the existing `SHIFT_LOCAL_AI_KEY_FILE`. The credential reader is unchanged: no pasted secrets, credential creation or credential setting changes. `SHIFT_EVAL_ROOT` is the immutable variant checkout; verify its exact commit and clean source before running. The report records hashes of prompt, jobs, history helper, provider, budget and public case. The caller-provided revision label alone is not proof of provenance. Raw output contains public fictional transcripts only and is not printed to stdout.

## Verification record

PR #50 is deployed as `5fbc2e159d795d66743578d098e08068e3e77059`, through [workflow 43141738227](https://github.com/tuliosoria/shift-the-timeline/actions/runs/43141738227). The served revision, health, AI mode and durable storage were verified. Its exact source passed 503 unit tests (one real-PostgreSQL test skipped in that job), all 25 PostgreSQL-job checks including the real database, 25 Python tests, syntax, dependency audit, AWS/Azure packaging, infrastructure compilation and all eight browser suites. Independent exact-head code review approved it.

The operator explicitly directed deployment and actual-game testing instead of obtaining a separate local credential. The hosted development comparison used the same fixed eight six-question conversations and legal revisits on baseline `857e06b` and candidate `5fbc2e1`: 48/48 AI replies in each, no fallbacks and 13 newly awarded cards in each. Two independently blinded reviewers each preferred the candidate in five conversations, tied three and preferred the baseline in none. Naturalness means improved from 3.25/5 to 4.13 and 4.38/5; this is a small qualitative sample, not a general failure-rate estimate or human enjoyment result. Polled median latency was 8.30 seconds baseline versus 6.53 seconds candidate. Each arm generated its own history; no poor response was selectively retried.

An additional eight candidate-only held-out conversations completed 48/48 AI replies without fallback. Their wording was sealed before source freeze; this set has no hosted baseline arm and must not be described as a paired improvement estimate. Independent factual review and the final phone playthrough are recorded separately in the campaign receipt.

The development reviews found one remaining medium factual flaw in the candidate: Rowan attributed his own permitted 'supplier muddle' excuse to Iona. The baseline also did this and separately contradicted the notice handover. A small follow-up adds a general speaker-attribution instruction: an instruction to call something a label is permission for the speaker's own wording, not evidence of someone else's words, belief or agreement. This follow-up was frozen from the development finding before held-out judgments were read. It changes no authored fact or case version. The comparison above measures PR #50, not the follow-up. Fresh hosted false-quotation and positive-attribution checks are required to assess the follow-up's effect; prompt-presence tests alone cannot establish model behaviour.

Remaining limits will include the unavailable private editorial case, no unspoiled human playtest, and statistical uncertainty from a small synthetic assistant-reviewed sample. Prompt instructions cannot guarantee truth, clue completeness or naturalness for arbitrary questions.
