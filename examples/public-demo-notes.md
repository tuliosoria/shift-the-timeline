## The Last Light at Bracken Quay

This is an original, deliberately public development mystery, not the private editorial case. The source contains the answer. Do not read these notes before a blind playtest. No production deployment, paid AI or private material is needed. Existing synthetic test fixtures remain separate.

The setting is a maritime museum on the afternoon before an opening. A forecast shows director Iona Vale dying beneath a suspended exhibition model at 18:00 tomorrow. Players arrive at 18:00 today, twenty-four hours before the forecast, after someone has knowingly cleared an unsafe movement but before it takes place. All preparation times from 15:00 to 17:55 refer to today. Three eight-hour game rounds reach tomorrow’s 18:00 opening. The presentation is non-graphic. The equipment and its safety workflow are fictional, with no real-world operating instructions.

## Solution and clue graph

**Rowan Calder**, the exhibition producer, diverted £10,000 and knows Iona will expose the payment trail after her speech. Rowan uses their existing personal clearance authority in the service booth to schedule the dangerous movement over her presentation mark.

The inference is an intersection, not a confession:

1. **Time and deliberate instruction:** intact event slips record the hold at 17:40, the sole clearance at 17:44, and sealing at 17:50. The failed load test was never superseded. Sources: Hugo `clue`, Milo `clue`, Wren `clue`.
2. **Authority and physical requirement:** only Rowan, Leena and Oscar can authorise movement; they must personally be at the booth. A borrowed card, remote command or override cannot do it. Sources: Ada `clue`, Leena `clue`, Oscar `clue`.
3. **Complete presence set:** only Rowan, Kit and Priya visit the initially empty booth during the entire relevant window. Sources: Basil `alibi` + `clue`, Gia `clue`, Priya `clue`. The recording and two observers corroborate the boundary checks and single entrance.
4. **Identity:** `{Rowan, Leena, Oscar} ∩ {Rowan, Kit, Priya} = {Rowan}`. Neither set on its own identifies anyone.
5. **Knowing danger and target:** the approval explicitly acknowledges the failed test and places the 18:00 tomorrow movement over Iona’s occupied centre mark. Sources: Hugo `motive`, Milo `motive`, Wren `motive`. Hugo `relationship` and Wren `motive` additionally establish receipt of the warning. Both running orders already place Iona there, so no unproved programme alteration is needed.
6. **Motive:** certified supplier/bank records identify the diverted payment and Rowan’s ownership of the receiving account. Sources: Cora `clue`, Nell `clue`, Tess `clue`. Their `motive` responses establish the notified disclosure after the speech.

The event record deliberately lacks operator identity; it does not solve the puzzle on its own. The personal-authorisation rule is an explicit fictional system guarantee, not an assumption that cards cannot be stolen. No culprit confession is required. Rowan admits ordinary access and evades the financial question, but supplies no unique indispensable fact.

## Red herrings and discovery

- **Oscar’s argument:** genuine but resolved at 15:00. Dev `clue`, Oscar `motive`, and the revised order prevent an indefinite grievance from masquerading as motive. Tess `alibi` independently places Oscar outside the booth window.
- **Kit’s booth access:** opportunity without clearance authority. Kit `alibi` explains the equipment card; the three independent authority lists exclude Kit. Kit `clue` explicitly warns that two photographs alone are not a continuous alibi.
- **Priya’s booth access:** likewise lacks the required authority. Her recording is useful evidence rather than a secret culpability cue.
- **Fees and launch pressure:** Ada `motive`, Basil `motive`, Dev `motive` and Gia `motive` establish a paid static preview. A delayed moving exhibit does not automatically ruin everyone financially.
- **Leena’s technical expertise:** authority without presence. Gia’s continuous rehearsal account and the complete doorway evidence resolve it; Kit’s photographs are supporting rather than conclusive.

Dossiers advertise records, vantage points and responsibilities rather than guilt. The producer is not first in the list, and no opening line names the criminal. Two optional side witnesses (Dev and Kit) can help resolve misleading impressions without being mandatory. Reinterviewing supplies the same facts; no character promises new testimony in a later round.

## Epistemic limits and editorial review

The deduction uses the specific constraints available in interviews: the intact event record, complete access interval, personal-authorisation rule, failed test and documented target position. A real investigation would independently verify them; this game presents those records and system guarantees as reliable fixed evidence. The notes do not add a universal honesty rule or turn a self-reported alibi into proof where independent evidence is needed. Nor does the intersection alone exclude an unmentioned co-conspirator: it identifies the person who issued the dangerous instruction. The authored solution contains one perpetrator, but its inference should not be advertised as a proof that no possible wider conspiracy exists.

The intersection establishes who issued the clearance under those premises. Knowing danger plus the financial exposure supports the intended crime; mathematical uniqueness of a person’s psychology cannot be proved from motive. The reveal must not rely on an additional confession, secret camera or unknown technical detail. Surviving payment copies also mean this is an attempt to delay exposure, not a magically successful destruction of all evidence.

The routes are highly redundant on purpose so missed interviews need not make the case unwinnable. That may make the identity deduction easy once players share the two sets. Blind testing should measure whether dossiers guide teams naturally, whether financial details are proportionate, and whether fixed topic labels make essential facts easy to find. The fictional personal-clearance mechanism and repeated record summaries deserve particular editorial scrutiny. This is a playable development case, not a claim of professionally calibrated mystery difficulty.
